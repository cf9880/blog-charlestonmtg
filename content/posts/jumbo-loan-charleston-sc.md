## What Is a Jumbo Loan?

A **jumbo loan** is a mortgage that's too large to be bought by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac. Those two companies can only buy loans up to the **conforming loan limit** set each year by the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA).

For 2026, [FHFA set the baseline limit](https://www.fhfa.gov/news/news-release/fhfa-announces-conforming-loan-limit-values-for-2026) at **$832,750** for a one-unit property, up from $806,500 in 2025. The Charleston area uses the baseline limit. FHFA typically announces the next year's limits in late November, so watch for 2027 numbers soon.

Because jumbo loans aren't backed by Fannie or Freddie, each lender or investor writes its own guidelines. That's why jumbo terms can vary more from lender to lender than conforming loans do.

The **loan amount**. You can buy a $1 million home with a conforming loan if your down payment brings the loan to $832,750 or less.

## What Are Typical Jumbo Loan Requirements?

Every lender is different, but many jumbo programs look at:

- **Down payment:** Often 10% to 20% or more, depending on the loan size and the program.
- **Credit score:** Usually higher than for many conforming loans.
- **Debt-to-income ratio (DTI):** Your monthly debts, including the new house payment, divided by gross monthly income. Many jumbo lenders prefer a lower DTI.
- **Cash reserves:** Money left over after closing, measured in months of house payments. Jumbo lenders often ask for several months.
- **Appraisal:** Some loan sizes require two appraisals.
- **Documentation:** Full income and asset documentation, though some lenders offer bank-statement or asset-based options for self-employed buyers.

## How Can I Get a Better Jumbo Loan Offer?

1. **Compare multiple lenders.** Jumbo pricing and rules vary widely, which is where a broker can help.
2. **Boost your down payment** if it gets you below $832,750 or into a better pricing tier.
3. **Keep reserves in documented accounts,** like checking, savings, or retirement accounts.
4. **Avoid new debt before closing,** especially during the holiday shopping season.
5. **Ask about ARMs and points.** A shorter fixed period or paying points may lower your rate, but compare the total cost.
6. **Check the market.** Our [Charleston housing market report for October 2026](/posts/charleston-housing-market-october-2026/) shows recent trends.
