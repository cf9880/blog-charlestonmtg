**Charleston County property taxes on 2026 bills are due January 15, 2027, without penalty. If you own and live in your home, you may qualify for South Carolina's 4% legal residence assessment ratio instead of 6%, but it isn't automatic: you must apply to the County Assessor, typically by January 15.**

Thanksgiving is when a lot of Lowcountry homeowners finally open the tax bill that arrived in October and start planning for the new year. This guide explains how Charleston County property taxes work, how the 4% rate can lower your bill, what happens with your mortgage escrow, and the deadlines to put on your calendar.

## Key Takeaways

- **Due date:** 2026 Charleston County real property taxes are due by **January 15, 2027**.
- **Penalties stack up:** 3% after January 15, 10% total after February 1, and 15% total after mid-March, per the county Treasurer.
- **4% vs. 6%:** an approved primary home is assessed at 4% of taxable value; other homes are assessed at 6%.
- **You must apply** for the 4% rate. The deadline is typically January 15 of each year.
- **Escrow matters:** if your lender pays your taxes, a late 4% approval can cause escrow adjustments.
- **Seniors and disabled owners** may qualify for a homestead exemption on the first $50,000 of value.

## When Are Charleston County Property Taxes Due?

Charleston County's Treasurer collects real estate taxes. Bills typically go out the first week of October, according to the County Assessor, and are due by January 15 of the following year.

| If you pay... | Penalty on 2026 bill |
|---|---|
| By January 15, 2027 | None |
| After January 15 | 3% |
| After February 1 | 10% total |
| After mid-March | 15% total, and the bill goes to the Delinquent Tax Department |

*Source: [Charleston County Treasurer's Office](https://www.charlestoncounty.gov/departments/treasurer/) real estate tax FAQs. Confirm exact dates on your bill.*

The Treasurer's office can't accept partial payment of a tax bill, according to Cobalt Prosper's summary of county rules, so plan for the full amount.

## What Is the 4% Legal Residence Rate?

South Carolina taxes homes based on an **assessment ratio**, a percentage of the home's taxable value that the tax rate (millage) is applied to.

- **4% ratio:** for an owner-occupied home that is your legal residence (domicile).
- **6% ratio:** for second homes, vacation homes, and most rentals.

Here's how much that changes the assessed value on the same home:

| Taxable value | Assessed at 4% | Assessed at 6% |
|---|---|---|
| $300,000 | $12,000 | $18,000 |
| $450,000 | $18,000 | $27,000 |
| $750,000 | $30,000 | $45,000 |

*Illustrative math only. Your tax bill also depends on your district's millage, exemptions, and fees.*

At 6%, the assessed value is 50% higher than at 4% on the same home, before any other credits. That's why getting the 4% rate right is one of the most important steps for a new Charleston homeowner.

### How to tell if your bill is at 4%

The Charleston County Assessor says to look at the **Assessment Ratio** box at the top of your bill. If it says **"QR4,"** your property is at 4%.

## How Do You Apply for the 4% Rate in Charleston County?

The 4% rate is not automatic. You must apply and prove the home is your legal residence.

1. **Apply online or by paper form** through the [Charleston County Assessor](https://www.charlestoncounty.gov/departments/assessor/).
2. **Provide proof of residence.** The application lists the documents the Assessor requires to show the home is your domicile.
3. **File as early as possible.** The Assessor recommends spring to early summer. The deadline is typically January 15.
4. **Don't wait to pay.** If your application isn't approved before the bill is due, pay the bill as issued. An approved application triggers an automatic refund.

The Assessor notes that applications surge after bills go out in October, which creates backlogs. If you bought this fall, apply now rather than in January.

### Who doesn't qualify?

According to Charleston County's Legal Residence application:

- A home **rented more than 72 days** in a calendar year doesn't qualify for that year.
- A home used for **vacation rentals at purchase** doesn't qualify that year if the owner didn't move in within 90 days.
- For a duplex or triplex, only units occupied by **immediate family members** qualify; rented portions are taxed at a partial 4%/6% split.
- Up to **five acres** may qualify with the home.

Missed it in a prior year? State law allows refund requests for up to two years if the home was eligible, the Assessor says.

## How Does Escrow Work With Property Taxes?

Most homeowners with a mortgage pay property taxes through an **escrow account**. Your lender collects part of the yearly tax bill with each monthly payment, then pays the county when the bill is due.

- **If your bill is at 6% when it should be 4%,** your escrow may be collecting too much. After approval, a new bill is issued, and your servicer adjusts.
- **If you just bought,** your lender may have estimated taxes at 6% until your 4% is approved. Ask your loan officer how it was set up.
- **Watch for an escrow analysis** in early 2027. A big change in your tax bill can raise or lower your monthly payment.

The Assessor warns that waiting to file can lead to large escrow adjustments. Filing early helps your bill go to your escrow company correctly.

## Are There Other Property Tax Breaks in Charleston?

- **Homestead exemption:** homeowners who are **65 or older, totally and permanently disabled, or legally blind** may exempt the first **$50,000** of fair market value on their legal residence. You must have the 4% ratio and apply through the County Auditor.
- **Disabled veterans:** veterans rated 100% permanently and totally disabled from a service-connected condition may qualify for a full exemption on their home. See our [VA loan Charleston guide](/posts/va-loan-charleston-sc/) for details.
