## What Do Charleston Agents Need From a Lender?

Lowcountry transactions often include details that do not show up in a simple rate shop:

- **Flood insurance and elevation certificates**, which can change the monthly payment and cash to close. See our guide to [buying in a Charleston flood zone](/posts/charleston-flood-zone-home-buying/).
- **Historic homes and inspection lists**, especially south of the Crosstown or in older village neighborhoods. Our post on [buying a historic home in Charleston](/posts/buy-historic-home-charleston/) covers some of the review issues.
- **Jumbo financing** when the price is above the conforming limit. Our [jumbo loan guide for Charleston](/posts/jumbo-loan-charleston-sc/) explains the 2026 limit and how jumbo underwriting differs.
- **VA buyers** tied to Joint Base Charleston or veterans moving to the area. See our [VA loan guide for Charleston](/posts/va-loan-charleston-sc/).

Agents need a loan officer who can talk through those items on a weeknight, rewrite a preapproval if the property type changes, and stay reachable through the inspection period.

## How Does the Loan Limit Affect Offers?

FHFA set the 2026 conforming loan limit at **$832,750** for a one-unit home in most U.S. counties, including the core Charleston-area counties. If the loan amount is higher than that, the buyer generally needs a jumbo program. A preapproval written as if the loan were conforming, when the price clearly is not, does not help a listing agent.

A mortgage broker can compare jumbo and conforming options so the offer letter matches the house. That is one reason agents send buyers to a broker instead of waiting until the appraisal to discover a limit problem.
