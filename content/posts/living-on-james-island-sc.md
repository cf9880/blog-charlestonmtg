## What Is It Like Living on James Island SC?

James Island feels residential and relaxed, with quick access to the city. Many neighborhoods back up to tidal creeks and marsh, and you'll find everything from 1950s ranch homes to newer construction and townhomes.

The island is a patchwork of jurisdictions. According to the [Town of James Island](https://www.jamesislandsc.us/history), residents first moved to form a town in 1992, and it was successfully incorporated in 2012. Other parts of the island are in the City of Charleston or unincorporated Charleston County. That matters for buyers, because your address determines which services, rules, and property taxes apply. Your agent and closing attorney can confirm which one applies to a home.

## What History Can You Find on James Island?

- **Fort Johnson:** The Town of James Island notes that the first shot of the Civil War was fired from Fort Johnson on April 12, 1861, signaling the attack on Fort Sumter.
- **McLeod Plantation Historic Site:** Run by Charleston County Parks, this site focuses on the lives and legacy of the enslaved people and Gullah Geechee community connected to it.
- **James Island County Park:** A 643-acre county park with trails, playgrounds, boat rentals, and a seasonal water park.

## When Is the Holiday Festival of Lights?

According to Charleston County Parks, the 2026 [Holiday Festival of Lights](https://www.ccprc.com/) runs **nightly from November 13 through December 31, from 5:30 to 10 p.m.**, at James Island County Park, 871 Riverland Drive. The drive-through route features more than 750 light displays, and the festival has been a Lowcountry tradition since 1990. Runners can join the festival's fun run on November 11 and 12.

Touring homes during the day and driving the lights at night is a fun way to get a feel for the island.

## What Should Buyers Watch For on James Island?

1. **Flood zones.** Low-lying, marsh-front lots may require flood insurance. Check the FEMA flood map and get an insurance quote early. Our [Charleston flood zone guide](/posts/charleston-flood-zone-home-buying/) explains how it works.
2. **Elevation and drainage.** Ask about the home's elevation certificate and any past water issues.
3. **Jurisdiction.** Confirm whether the home is in the Town, the City, or the county.
4. **Commute.** Traffic on the connector and Folly Road can be heavy at rush hour, so test-drive your commute.
5. **Holiday timing.** Buying between Thanksgiving and New Year's can mean fewer competing buyers. See our [Charleston winter home buying guide](/posts/charleston-winter-home-buying-guide/).
