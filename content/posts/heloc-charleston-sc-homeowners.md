**A HELOC lets Charleston homeowners borrow against their equity without giving up a low first-mortgage rate. Most lenders let you borrow up to about 80% of your home's value, minus what you owe. The national average HELOC rate was 7.36% on October 21, 2026, according to Bankrate, and HELOC rates usually move with the Fed.**

With Charleston County single-family prices near $715,000, many local owners have built real equity. If you want to fund a renovation, storm hardening, or a big expense this fall, here's how to decide whether a HELOC is the right tool.

## Key Takeaways

- **Keep your low rate:** a HELOC is a second loan, so your first mortgage stays the same.
- **Borrowing limit:** most lenders cap combined loans at about 80% of value; some go to 85%–90%.
- **Rates are variable:** Bankrate's national average was 7.36% on October 21, 2026, after five straight weekly increases.
- **Interest may be deductible** only if you use the money to buy, build, or substantially improve the home.
- **Charleston equity is real:** Charleston County's September single-family median was $715,000, up 1.6% from a year ago.

## How Much Can You Borrow With a HELOC?

Here's a simple example using an 80% combined loan-to-value (CLTV) limit:

| Step | Example |
|---|---|
| Home value (appraised) | $600,000 |
| 80% of value | $480,000 |
| Minus current mortgage balance | −$330,000 |
| **Possible HELOC line** | **$150,000** |

Your actual line depends on the appraisal, your credit, your income and debts, and each lender's limits. Most HELOCs have a **draw period** (often 10 years) when you can borrow and may pay interest only, followed by a **repayment period** when payments rise to cover principal.

## HELOC vs. Home Equity Loan vs. Cash-Out Refinance

| Option | Rate type | Best when |
|---|---|---|
| HELOC | Usually variable | You'll borrow over time, like a phased renovation |
| Home equity loan | Usually fixed | You need one lump sum and want a set payment |
| Cash-out refinance | New first mortgage | Your current rate is close to today's rates, or you need to pay off a large balance |

With the average 30-year fixed at **7.33%** on October 26 (Mortgage News Daily), a cash-out refinance would replace your whole mortgage at today's rate. If your current rate is much lower, a second loan is usually the better math.

Bankrate's national average HELOC rate was **7.36%** as of October 21, 2026, with offers ranging from 3.99% to 11.85% depending on the lender and borrower. ## Is HELOC Interest Tax-Deductible?

According to [IRS Publication 936](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p936), interest on home equity debt is generally deductible only if the money is used to **buy, build, or substantially improve** the home that secures the loan. It also counts toward the $750,000 limit on qualifying mortgage debt ($375,000 if married filing separately). Using a HELOC to pay off credit cards does not qualify. Talk with your tax advisor.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a HELOC?

A home equity line of credit is a revolving line secured by your home. You draw what you need during the draw period, pay interest on what you use, and repay the balance over time.

### How much can I borrow with a HELOC in Charleston?

Many lenders allow combined loans up to about 80% of your home's value, and some go to 85% to 90%. Subtract your mortgage balance from that amount to estimate your line.

### What is the average HELOC rate right now?

Bankrate reported a national average of 7.36% as of October 21, 2026, with offers from 3.99% to 11.85%. Your rate depends on credit, equity, and the lender.

### Will a HELOC change my first mortgage rate?

No. A HELOC is a separate second loan, so your existing first mortgage and its rate stay the same.

### Is HELOC interest tax-deductible?

Per IRS Publication 936, generally only if the funds are used to buy, build, or substantially improve the home that secures the loan, within the $750,000 debt limit. Check with a tax advisor.

### Should I get a HELOC or a cash-out refinance?

If your current rate is well below today's 30-year average of 7.33%, a HELOC or home equity loan usually keeps your costs lower. A cash-out refinance may make sense if your rate is already near today's rates.
